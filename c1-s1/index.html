C1-S1 The shortlist challenge

A working demonstration of one scenario from the Course 1 scenario bank, set in a Singapore logistics SME: Daniel Lim, the hiring manager, speaks to the learner on screen as a photoreal, lip-synced avatar; the learner answers; the scenario engine scores the conversation against the IHRP performance statements, keeps the record, and the facilitator validates the reflection.

The situation

Harbourline Logistics Pte Ltd (fictional) is a home-grown Singapore logistics company: 180 staff, a distribution centre in Tuas, customers in retail and FMCG. It is hiring an Operations Planner for the Tuas planning team. The job was advertised on the national jobs portal; 80 people applied. The AI screening tool the company trialled this year has produced a ranked list, and you have the shortlisting matrix scores for the four strongest applicants.

Daniel Lim, Head of Operations Planning (Singaporean, 46, twelve years in logistics operations), wants to interview Marcus Tan (a former colleague from his previous employer, "culture fit", AI match 92%) and one other, and set aside Nurul Aisyah (a qualified Singaporean candidate, AI match 71%). He has given you five minutes in Meeting Room 3B before his Tuas operations call.

Your role
HR business partner for Operations. You own the shortlisting process.
Counterpart
Daniel Lim, hiring manager. Impatient, results-driven, not hostile. He respects evidence once he sees it. On screen he is a photoreal avatar: an AI-generated likeness of a fictional person (no real individual), lip-synced to each line, with his expression following the mood of the line.
Your task
Agree a defensible shortlist: skills-first criteria applied the same way to everyone, fair consideration of local candidates under the Fair Consideration Framework and the Tripartite Guidelines, and a clear line on what the AI screening score can and cannot be relied on for.
Time
10 to 15 minutes in the live version; 4 to 6 minutes in guided practice.
Strategic Enterprise lens
Trust and fairness: criteria that candidates and managers can trust. Structure: who decides, and on what evidence.

Learner guide: scenario-based role play in Canvas

You will find the scenarios inside your SIM Academy Canvas course, in the module for each unit. Each scenario is a short conversation with a realistic counterpart who speaks to you on screen. You can practise it as often as you like. Your best attempt counts, and your facilitator validates it.

Access

  1. Sign in to CanvasUse your SIM Academy learner account on a laptop or tablet (Chrome or Edge). Use earphones in a shared office; a headset if you want to speak your answers.
  2. Open the course"AI for HR Programme: Workplace Fairness and Inclusivity with AI".
  3. Go to ModulesOpen Unit 1 · Skills-first shortlisting and click Scenario C1-S1: The shortlist challenge.
  4. Click Open scenarioIt opens inside Canvas. No second login: Canvas tells the scenario who you are.

Complete

  1. Read the brief and the evidence packTwo minutes: the situation, your role, what good looks like, the matrix scores.
  2. Start attemptChoose guided practice or the live AI counterpart. Daniel speaks; captions are always on. Answer by choosing, typing, or (live version) pressing Speak and confirming the transcript. 10 to 15 minutes.
  3. End the conversationRead your feedback against the three performance statements, and the strong move for any turn that fell short.
  4. Not attained? Try againWrite one line on what you will do differently, then start again. Every attempt is kept; your best one counts.

Record your reflection

  1. Open "C1-S1 Self-reflection"The Canvas assignment next to the scenario. Answer the three prompts (150 to 250 words in total).
  2. Tick the declaration and submitYour own work; no real employee or candidate data. Status becomes "Submitted, awaiting validation".
  3. Facilitator validatesYour facilitator reads your scenario record and reflection, then marks the scenario Validated, or returns it with a comment and asks for one more attempt.
  4. Recorded as evidenceA validated scenario goes into your course evidence record (E1 for Unit 1). The Oral Questioning assessor sees it and confirms it; you are not assessed on it again.

Practising more than once

  • Unlimited attempts, during the course and up to the day of your booked assessment. There is no penalty for a failed attempt; it stays on your record as part of the learning path.
  • Feedback after every attempt, in the same language the assessor uses. For each turn that fell short you see the strong move, so the next attempt starts from what you learned.
  • Daniel varies. His opening and his objections rotate between attempts (and the live AI version never repeats itself), so repeating the scenario is practice, not memorising a script.
  • Your note carries forward. The line you wrote after your last attempt is shown to you at the start of the next one.
  • Your best attempt counts. Attainment is recorded when an attempt meets all three statements. Validation needs your submitted reflection as well.
  • Before the assessment, practise the scenario you found hardest once more in the week before your booked Oral Questioning slot.

What counts, and who decides

  • The software scores practice. A person makes every decision. Your facilitator validates attainment; the ACTA/ACLP certified assessor confirms it in the Oral Questioning assessment.
  • Validated means: an attempt that met all three statements, plus a reflection that names what changed between attempts, links it to a statement in your words, and states one workplace application.
  • Nothing real goes in. All names, scores and documents are fictional. Do not type or say real employee or candidate details in any scenario.
  • If you cannot use a browser-based scenario (device, access, disability, language), your facilitator runs the same scenario with you live, from the same brief, with no disadvantage.
  • Help: the facilitator in class; between sessions, the course inbox in Canvas.

Hand-off to the assessor

Before the booked assessment the assessor receives each learner's course evidence record, including validated scenarios with the attempt summary and reflection. In the Oral Questioning assessment the assessor confirms the statements already evidenced and spends the time on what is not yet evidenced. Scenario records and reflections are kept for moderation (second-assessor sampling of 10% per cohort) for the period agreed with SIM, then deleted.
